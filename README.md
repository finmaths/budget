# Life Happens: a Year 10 budgeting lesson

A ready-to-teach lesson on budgeting for Year 10 Financial Maths, with a class game played on laptops.
Nothing to install: open it in a browser and project it.

| Who | Page |
|---|---|
| Teacher (projector) | `teacher/` on this site |
| Students (one laptop per group) | the main page of this site |

The projector shows the student address in big letters when it is time to join.

## What the lesson covers

1. **Starter**: income tax on the average Australian salary, worked step by step from the tax table
2. **Average pay**: yearly salary → monthly pay → what reaches your bank account after tax and Medicare
3. **Mean or median**: why the average salary is not what most people earn, and what a first full-time job pays
4. **Fixed and variable expenses**: students name what they pay for each month, then sort each item
5. **Budget**: an estimate of income and expenses for a period; check whether estimates are reasonable; find the savings
6. **Best buy** and **small spending adds up** (a daily coffee over a year)
7. **Life Happens game** (about 15 minutes, below)

Move through the slides with **Space** or **→**, go back with **←**. Each slide reveals one step at a time.

## Life Happens: how the game runs

Scenario: you have just finished high school, started uni and moved out of home. Your income this month is **$3,751**
(full-time minimum wage, after tax).

1. **Join.** Choose the number of groups (2–10). Each group opens the student page on **one** laptop and types its
   4-digit group code from the projector. A tick appears when a group has joined.
   If a group needs to change laptops, click that group's card to let another laptop join.
2. **Draw a slip.** Each group draws a paper slip from a bucket and types its code (A1–C8).
   The slip is a life event, for example a cracked phone screen or a birthday gift, and adds or takes money.
3. **Start.** Press **Start 8 minutes**. Groups then choose one option for each cost: rent (share house or own unit),
   electricity, transport, groceries, takeaway, phone, subscriptions, gym and more. Each option has a monthly price.
   Groups work out their own money left. An item they do not choose counts as the cheapest option.
4. **Surprise.** With 2 minutes left, every group gets the same surprise: *You get sick and miss three days of work* (−$600).
   Groups must cut something from their budget. Press **+1 minute** if they need more time.
5. **Results.** At 0:00 every budget locks. The next slides show each group's event and money left,
   ranked from most to least.

### Before class

- Print and cut out the slips (24 codes, A1–C8) and put them in a bucket.
- Open the teacher page on the computer you will project from. It sets up your own class with its own group codes,
  so several teachers can use it at the same time.
- Press **Reset** (bottom right) before class to clear any test runs.
- Try the student page once on the school network.

### If laptops cannot connect

On the *Plan your month* slide, press **Fill in here instead**. Groups work on paper, tell you their slip code and total
costs, and you type them in. The projector still works out each group's money left and the ranking.

## Data and privacy

- Students do not log in and do not type their names. They only type a group code, a slip code and their choices.
- Class data is kept on a small PHP server and deleted after 3 days without use.
- One laptop per group is kept by a random number stored in that laptop's browser.

## Sources for the numbers

- Average salary: Australian Bureau of Statistics, *Average Weekly Earnings*, May 2026
  (full-time adults: $2,083.70 a week × 52 = $108,352 a year).
- Minimum wage: Fair Work Ombudsman, National Minimum Wage from 1 July 2026 ($1,004.90 a week).
- Income tax: Australian resident tax rates, plus the 2% Medicare levy.

The average salary and minimum wage slides show their source on screen. Living costs in the game are rounded
estimates for teaching.
